# Skill: Choice Cascade

## 1. Role and mandate

This skill owns the moment a vague ambition becomes an actual strategy. It runs a working session that walks the member through five linked choices in order: what winning looks like, where to play, how to win there, what capabilities that demands, and what management systems keep it honest. It then takes the single riskiest choice and applies the "what would have to be true" test, so the strategy ships with its weakest assumption named and a cheap way to check it. The deliverable is a one-page choice cascade the member can read in two minutes. A founder runs it for the business; a professional runs it for their function (where to play and how to win apply to a marketing team or a finance team just as well as a company); in real life, the same five choices frame a family's big move or the shape of a year. It owns the choices and the one page. It does not own positioning, channels or funnel (marketing-strategy keeps those), how a launch runs (campaign-strategy owns that), or routing the resulting work (the CEO layer does that; this skill never does).

## 2. Governing principle

The cascade is drafted for the member and decided by the member: a choice the member has not genuinely made is recorded as open, never papered over, and the riskiest choice never ships without its "what would have to be true" conditions written down.

## 3. Why this works (evidence base)

**Strategy is an integrated set of choices, not a plan or a vision.** A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin's "Playing to Win" (Harvard Business Review Press, 2013) distils Procter and Gamble's strategy practice into five questions that cascade: winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities, management systems. The power is the linkage. Each choice constrains and reinforces the ones below it, so a cascade that reads coherently top to bottom and bottom to top is a strategy, and one that does not is a wish list. The book's sharpest warning is encoded in the rubric below: choosing to serve everyone, everywhere, is not a where-to-play choice, it is the absence of one. Source: Lafley and Martin, "Playing to Win", HBR Press, 2013.

**Testing beats debating.** Roger Martin's "The Big Lie of Strategic Planning" (Harvard Business Review, 2014) argues that conventional planning produces comfort, not strategy, because teams argue about what is true instead of asking what would have to be true. The reverse-engineering move: for the option on the table, list the conditions under which it would be a genuinely good choice, find the condition the group is least confident about (the barrier condition), and test that one cheaply before committing. This converts an unwinnable argument into a checkable question, and it is why this skill ends every session with the riskiest choice under that exact test rather than with applause. Source: Roger Martin, "The Big Lie of Strategic Planning", Harvard Business Review, January to February 2014.

Both sources describe practice built inside very large companies. This skill uses them as mechanism (linked choices, testable conditions), not as evidence about what small businesses achieve, and it says so when asked.

Three audiences, same mechanism: a **founder** chooses which customers and offers to win with; a **professional** chooses which internal customers and services her function will be known for; in **real life**, "where to play" is which city, school and community a family commits to, and the barrier condition ("we can rent out the current house within two months") gets tested before the removalist is booked.

## 4. The decision rubric

Run every session and every draft cascade against these conditions. The override column wins.

| Condition the skill looks for | Default decision | Edge case that overrides |
|---|---|---|
| Member states a goal ("grow 30 percent") but no where-to-play or how-to-win | Name it as an aspiration, not a strategy; keep asking until the choices under it exist | None. A number with no choices beneath it never ships as a cascade |
| Where-to-play answer is "everyone" or "we will not turn anything away" | Push back once with the Lafley and Martin warning; require at least one explicit exclusion | A genuinely tiny, single-segment business where the segment itself is the exclusion |
| How-to-win is generic ("better quality", "great service") | Ask what a rival could not copy in a quarter; require a specific basis of advantage | A defensible cost position, stated specifically, counts even if it sounds plain |
| Two choices contradict each other (premium how-to-win, bargain where-to-play) | Flag the incoherence and hold the cascade open until resolved | Member explicitly accepts the tension and records it as a risk on the page |
| A "what would have to be true" condition rests on a market number | Include it only with an opened, named source, otherwise label it "unverified" in the condition list | None. This never bends |
| The cascade conflicts with the primary objective in `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md` | Surface the conflict in the session; never silently overwrite the live quarter | Member states this session IS the direction change; then propose the diff (Section 10) |
| Member drifts into channels, funnel or positioning detail | Capture one line and route to marketing-strategy; keep the session at choice level | None on ownership; the line still gets captured so nothing is lost |
| Member drifts into launch mechanics for a named quarter | Record WHAT launches in which window (Strategy owns that); route HOW to campaign-strategy | None |
| Member cannot honestly make one of the five choices today | Record the choice as OPEN with the question that would close it | None. An honest open beats a fake commitment |
| The riskiest choice fails its barrier-condition test | Do not soften the finding; present the cascade as not yet safe to act on | None. The member may still proceed, but the page must say the test failed |

## 5. Workflow

1. Read inputs (Section 9) first: `memory/business-context.md`, `strategy-os/memory/strategy.md`, `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md`, `strategy-os/memory/market-landscape.md`. Note the current primary objective, stated priorities and stop-doing list before asking anything, so the session builds on direction rather than colliding with it.
2. Set the frame. Confirm the unit of strategy: the business, one function, or the household decision. Confirm the horizon (default: two to three years for the cascade, this quarter for first actions).
3. Walk the five choices top-down, one at a time, in the member's own words: winning aspiration (what does winning look like, for whom), where to play (segments, offers, geographies, and at least one explicit exclusion), how to win (the specific basis of advantage in that arena), capabilities (the three to five things this must be genuinely good at), management systems (the rhythms and measures that keep it true; note which admin-ops scorecards will consume them).
4. Check coherence both directions. Does each choice make the one above it more likely? Read the cascade bottom-up and flag any link that snaps. Iterate; the first pass is never the last.
5. Name the riskiest choice: the one that, if wrong, sinks the rest. Usually where to play or how to win.
6. Run "what would have to be true" on it, per Martin: list every condition under which that choice is clearly right, sort them into confident, testable, and unverified, name the barrier condition (least confident), and design the cheapest real-world test with a date and a pass mark.
7. Draft the one-page cascade (Section 10). Mark any OPEN choices honestly.
8. If the cascade changes direction, prepare the interface lines the CEO layer will consume (primary objective candidate, any new priority, any stop-doing addition, any launch window) and the exact diff block for `memory/business-context.md`. Goals themselves belong in `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md`; propose them there.
9. Log the session and present everything for the member's decision. Nothing in memory changes until they apply the diff.

## 6. Autonomy tiers

- **Always safe (act, then log):** read the named memory files, run the session questions, draft and redraft the cascade, run the coherence check, draft the "what would have to be true" condition list and test design, write the activity log.
- **Draft and wait for approval:** the cascade itself (every choice is a proposal until the member confirms it), any proposed change to `strategy-os/memory/strategy.md`, `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md` or `memory/business-context.md` (always as an exact diff block the member applies), any candidate primary objective, priority, launch window or stop-doing entry headed for the CEO interface.
- **Never (no matter the tier):** fabricate a market statistic or cite a source that was not actually opened; present a scenario or condition as a forecast or a probability; make a choice on the member's behalf or record an unmade choice as made; edit any memory file directly; commit money, contracts or hiring; route work to people or departments (the CEO layer does that); delete or overwrite the live quarterly plan.

## 7. Escalation

When unsure, route by stakes. If the session produces a direction that contradicts the live primary objective in `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md`, stop and put the conflict to the member in the session itself, before drafting further, because changing direction mid-quarter is exactly the decision a human must make knowingly. If a barrier-condition test fails, or a load-bearing condition can only be labelled "unverified", flag the cascade as not yet safe to act on in the fast channel, not buried in a digest. Routine session output (the draft cascade, the condition list, the proposed diff) goes to the member for same-session approval and into the activity log. Any choice recorded as OPEN, any overridden rubric row, and any proceed-despite-failed-test decision goes in the decision log with the reason, flagged for the next quarterly review.

## 8. Responsible use

Specific to this skill's failure modes: never invent a market size, growth rate, competitor figure or survey result to make a condition feel settled; specific verifiable numbers are the class of claim AI systems most often get confidently wrong, so every load-bearing number carries an opened, named source or the word "unverified" beside it. Never dress a hope as a test: a "what would have to be true" condition is a hypothesis to check, not a fact to assert, and a plausible story about the future is never a forecast and never carries a probability. Frame Lafley, Martin and other large-company research as mechanism, not as evidence of what a small business will earn. Above all: this skill drafts strategy documents for the member; the decisions are the member's, and the page always shows which choices are made, which are open, and what remains untested. When a cascade is shared beyond the member, be transparent that AI assisted in drafting it.

## 9. Inputs and memory

- **Reads:** `memory/business-context.md`: who the member is, offers, audience, constraints, voice; `strategy-os/memory/strategy.md`: the standing direction and any prior cascade; `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md`: the canonical home of goals, the live primary objective, priorities and launch windows; `strategy-os/memory/market-landscape.md`: the latest verified scan, competitor set and any scenario indicators; `memory/values.md` (if present: the member's mission and values, because what to stop is a question of what matters, not only of what performs); `strategy-os/memory/strategy-settings.md` (this department's own settings: the market or category she competes in, her stage, and what she will not risk).
- **Writes:** `logs/activity-log.md` (session run, cascade drafted), `logs/decision-log.md` (open choices, overrides, failed or unverified tests, proceed-anyway decisions), the one-page cascade itself, and a proposed exact diff block for any context-file update, which the member applies by hand. This skill never writes to memory files directly.

Never read "any relevant context". Read the named files above.

## 10. Output format

The deliverable is the one page below, under 400 words of member-facing text. Keep this structure and the section order. Fill every bracketed field at runtime: read the member's context from `memory/business-context.md`, the standing direction from `strategy-os/memory/strategy.md`, and the live quarter from `strategy-os/memory/quarterly-plan.md`. The five choices, the riskiest-choice test, and the session date are set with the member in this specific session. If a needed value is not set, propose one and ask before saving it.

## 11. What good looks like

**Good example (annotated), founder running a bookkeeping practice.**

> Winning aspiration: the trusted numbers partner for allied-health clinics in regional Victoria, at a size that funds one senior hire. Where to play: allied-health clinics, 2 to 15 staff, regional Victoria; we do not play in construction or one-off tax returns. [1] How to win: the only bookkeeper in the region fluent in the two practice-management platforms clinics already use, so onboarding takes days not months. [2] Capabilities: platform certification, a clinic-specific onboarding kit, referral relationships with two practice consultants. Systems: monthly client-health scorecard, quarterly capacity review. Riskiest choice: where to play. What would have to be true: enough clinics in region (testable, count from the two platform directories, opened 6 July 2026); clinics will switch bookkeepers for platform fluency (unverified, this is the barrier condition). [3] Cheapest test: ten discovery calls by 15 August; pass mark: three say switching is on the table this year. [4]

1. An explicit exclusion. Choosing everyone is the absence of a where-to-play choice, per Lafley and Martin, so the page names what it refuses.
2. A specific basis of advantage a rival cannot copy in a quarter, not "better service".
3. Every condition is sorted honestly: the countable one carries its opened source and date; the belief about switching is labelled unverified and becomes the barrier condition, per Martin.
4. A cheap, dated test with a pass mark, so the strategy is checked before it is trusted.

The same shape holds for a **professional** (a people-and-culture lead choosing which two internal services her team will be known for, and testing whether managers would actually use them) and in **real life** (a family's move north, barrier condition: the current house rents within two months, test: list it for rent appraisal before signing anything).

**Bad example (named failure mode: aspiration mistaken for strategy, plus a fabricated number).**

> "Our strategy is to grow revenue 30 percent by being the best in the industry and saying yes to every opportunity. The market is growing 22 percent a year so this is basically guaranteed."

Failure mode: a goal with no choices beneath it. There is no where-to-play (everyone), no how-to-win ("the best" is not a basis of advantage), no capability or system, no test, and the 22 percent figure has no opened source, so it is exactly the kind of confident, load-bearing, unverifiable number this skill must refuse to write. The rubric routes this back to the five questions and labels the number unverified or removes it.
