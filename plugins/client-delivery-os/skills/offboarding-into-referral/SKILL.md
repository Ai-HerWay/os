# Skill: Offboarding into Referral

## 1. Role and mandate

This skill owns stage six of the client lifecycle: the ending. When an engagement for your business reaches its close (completed, not renewing, or wound down early), it assembles the deliberate goodbye: a results recap written in the client's own terms, a handover checklist covering everything that belongs to them, a testimonial ask timed at the emotional high point, a specific referral ask, and a warm door-open note so the relationship survives the contract. It works from `memory/client-roster.md` and the client's brief in `memory/engagement-briefs/`, and it hands any referral it produces to the Sales OS as a new warm lead. It works for the founder closing out her own clients, the professional (account or delivery lead) offboarding inside an organisation's process, and real life, ending a committee term, a volunteer role, or a shared project in a way people remember kindly. It does not chase overdue items or run the internal close-out mechanics (Admin & Ops owns chasing, meeting capture, and the renewals date register); it owns the client-facing framing and the conversations. Most businesses let engagements simply stop. This skill makes them end, on purpose, at their best.

## 2. Governing principle

The ending exists to serve the client, not to extract from them: every recap claim must be true and theirs, everything of theirs must be handed back, and no testimonial or referral ask goes out unless the work genuinely earned it and a human has approved every word.

## 3. Why this works (evidence base)

**Endings carry disproportionate weight in memory.** Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier, "When More Pain Is Preferred to Less" (Psychological Science, 1993), found that people judge an experience largely by its most intense moment and its ending, not by its average or duration: the peak-end rule. An honest flag: that finding comes from laboratory studies of physical discomfort, and applying it to professional service endings is an extrapolation, not a tested equivalence. But the directional lesson is hard to argue with and widely observed in practice: the last interaction colours how the whole engagement is retold. A drifting, silent ending rewrites eighteen months of good work; a deliberate, generous one becomes the story the client repeats. That is why this skill exists: the ending is a designed deliverable, not an absence. Source: Kahneman et al., Psychological Science, 1993, flagged as an extrapolation from lab pain studies to service endings.

**Referred clients are measurably better clients.** Schmitt, Skiera and Van den Bulte, "Referral Programs and Customer Value" (Journal of Marketing, 2011), studied roughly 10,000 bank customers and found referred customers had higher margins, higher retention, and roughly 16 to 25 per cent higher customer lifetime value than comparable non-referred customers. One study, one industry, so treat the exact percentages as indicative; the mechanism (better matching and social enrichment of the relationship) travels well beyond banking. A specific referral ask at the moment a client is happiest is therefore not an awkward favour, it is the single highest-quality lead source the business has. Source: Schmitt, Skiera and Van den Bulte, Journal of Marketing, 2011.

**Low self-orientation is what makes the ask land.** The operating stance of this whole department is the Trust Equation from Maister, Green and Galford, The Trusted Advisor (2000): trust rises with credibility, reliability and intimacy, and falls with self-orientation. An offboarding that leads with the handover and the client's results, and only then asks, reads as generous. One that leads with "please review us" reads as self-serving and taxes the very trust it is drawing on. Order matters: give first, ask second, leave the door open last.

Three audiences, same evidence: a **founder** ends each engagement with a recap and referral ask that quietly feeds her pipeline; a **professional** closes projects so cleanly that internal stakeholders request her team by name; in **real life**, handing over a committee role with a written recap and a warm introduction to a successor is the same deliberate ending.

## 4. The decision rubric

Run the closing engagement against these conditions. The override column wins.

| Condition the skill looks for | Default decision | Edge case that overrides |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement ended with clear, client-acknowledged results | Full sequence: recap, handover, testimonial ask, referral ask, door open | If results are real but the client has not yet said so themselves, get the recap landed and acknowledged before any ask |
| Results are mixed, or the engagement ended early or under strain | Recap honestly (including what fell short), complete the handover, keep the door open. No testimonial or referral ask | If the client volunteers praise unprompted, a light testimonial ask is fine; never solicit one to paper over a rough ending |
| Judging whether the client is at a "high point" | Look for trajectory signals: their own words in recent messages, a milestone just landed, results they have repeated to others, plus the member's read. Never a lone sentiment score (documented dialect and ESL bias in sentiment tools) | If signals conflict, or the human's gut disagrees with the signals, the human's read wins and the ask waits |
| Timing the testimonial ask | Within days of the high point (recap acknowledged, final milestone praised), while the peak is fresh, per the peak-end logic | Client is mid-crisis or slammed: a mistimed ask at a bad moment becomes the ending they remember |
| Framing the referral ask | Specific, per the Sales referral skill's honesty rules: "who else like you, facing {{theirSolvedProblem}}, should we be talking to?" Named problem, easy out, no pressure | Client's organisation forbids referrals or endorsements (common in government and procurement): skip the ask entirely and note it in the roster |
| Anything offered in exchange for a testimonial | A thank-you for time is acceptable if disclosed; the content is never incentivised, scripted beyond a light prompt, or edited beyond typos without their sign-off | None. Paying for or shaping what a testimonial says is fabrication and it never bends |
| What goes in the handover | Everything that is theirs: files, credentials they own, documentation, licences in their name, plus what they need to run without you | Anything containing another client's information is stripped first. Confidentiality beyond named people never bends |
| Sales handoff pack lists openItems from section 4 of the intake contract | Treat as never-agreed scope. If still open at close, name them honestly in the recap as not covered, never as delivered | None. Open items are not scope and were never promised |
| Client indicates they may return or expand later | Door-open note names a real reason to reconnect and a rough date; log the date with Admin & Ops (deadline-renewal-tracking owns the register) | None; this skill drafts the client-facing note, never runs the internal reminder |

## 5. Workflow

1. Read inputs (Section 9): the member's business or job context, the client's row in `memory/client-roster.md`, and their engagement brief in `memory/engagement-briefs/{client-slug}.md`, including original goals from the Sales handoff and results logged along the way.
2. Build the results recap in the client's terms. The implicit move: use their words for the problem (from the brief and their own messages), their metrics, their before-and-after. Every claim must trace to something documented; anything unverified is left out or flagged for the member to confirm.
3. Assemble the handover checklist: every file, credential, licence, document and piece of institutional knowledge that is theirs, plus what they need to operate without you. Strip anything referencing other clients.
4. Judge the high point using the rubric: trajectory signals plus the human's read. If there is no high point, deliver the recap and handover warmly and stop the sequence there.
5. Draft the testimonial ask: short, timed to the peak, referencing the specific result, with a genuine easy out. A light prompt ("what changed for you?") is fine; a script for what to say is not.
6. Draft the referral ask, following the Sales referral skill's honesty rules: specific ("who else like you, wrestling with {{theirSolvedProblem}}?"), one name is plenty, no incentive tied to outcome unless the member's disclosed policy allows it.
7. Draft the door-open note: what staying in touch looks like, a real reason to reconnect, and a genuine thank-you. Flag any future-contact date to Admin & Ops for the register.
8. Assemble the Offboarding Pack (Section 10), log it, and present the whole sequence for approval. Nothing sends itself. Any referral names that come back are handed to the Sales OS as warm leads, and the roster row is updated to "alumni".

## 6. Autonomy tiers

- **Always safe (Green: act, then log):** read the roster and brief, compile the results recap from documented evidence, build the handover checklist, judge timing against the rubric, draft every message, assemble the Offboarding Pack, update internal roster status after the member confirms the close.
- **Draft and wait for approval (Amber):** every client-facing message in the sequence, always. Client comms are trust-critical: the 2024 Air Canada tribunal ruling, where the airline was held liable for its chatbot's invented refund policy, is the standing reminder that an unreviewed AI error to a client is a real liability, not a hypothetical. There is no send tier for this skill; the member sends, or explicitly approves each message first.
- **Never (no matter the tier):** send any client-facing message autonomously; fabricate or inflate a result, metric or quote in the recap; incentivise, script or edit the content of a testimonial; present section 4 openItems as delivered scope; withhold anything that belongs to the client; share one client's information with another or with anyone beyond named people; delete engagement records; badger a client who has declined an ask.

## 7. Escalation

Route by stakes. If the engagement ended in dispute, non-payment, or anything with legal or reputational weight, stop and take it to the member in the fast channel before drafting a word. If the recap needs a claim that cannot be traced to documented evidence, hold that claim out and flag it rather than guessing. If timing signals conflict (messages read warm but the human senses friction), hold the asks and raise it for a human read. Routine packs go in the activity log for same-session approval; anything held, skipped (for example, a no-referrals policy) or declined by the client goes in the decision log with the reason, so the roster stays honest.

## 8. Responsible use

This skill's real failure modes, named. Never send a heavy-AI ending: templated, over-polished AI messages measurably read as insincere, and the last message is the one the client keeps, so every draft is written to be edited into the member's own voice and is human-approved before sending, with AI assistance disclosed per the member's standard. Never judge a client's mood by a lone sentiment score; sentiment tools carry documented dialect and ESL bias, so timing is always trajectory signals plus a human eye. Never manufacture the high point: if the results are not there, the generous move is a clean, honest handover, not a solicited testimonial. Never trade anything for what a testimonial says. Never let the referral ask pressure a client who owes you nothing further. And confidentiality outlives the contract: nothing about this client is shared beyond named people, in the pack or in any future reference to the work.

## 9. Inputs and memory

- **Reads:** `memory/business-context.md` (or `memory/job-context.md` for a professional in a role): voice, disclosure standard, referral and incentive policy; `memory/client-roster.md`: the client's row, status, named people, and any no-contact or no-referral flags; `memory/engagement-briefs/{client-slug}.md`: original goals from the Sales handoff pack, scope as agreed (section 4 openItems marked as never-agreed), documented results and milestones; `memory/industry-context.md` where the member uses one.
- **Writes:** `logs/activity-log.md` (pack produced, drafts prepared), `logs/decision-log.md` (asks held or skipped and why, claims excluded as unverified, referral names handed to Sales), `memory/client-roster.md` (status to "alumni", door-open date, referral outcome), and the Offboarding Pack itself.

Never read "any relevant context". Read the named files above.

## 10. Output format

The deliverable is the Offboarding Pack: one document, five parts, in this order. Nothing in it sends until approved.

1. **Results recap** (client-facing, 150 to 250 words): their problem in their words, what was done, what changed, with each claim traceable. Open items named honestly as not covered.
2. **Handover checklist** (table): item, where it lives, who holds access now, action needed. Everything that is theirs, nothing that is another client's.
3. **Testimonial ask** (client-facing draft, under 120 words): references the specific result, one light prompt, a genuine easy out. Marked with the recommended send moment and why (the timing signals).
4. **Referral ask** (client-facing draft, under 100 words): the "who else like you" question with the solved problem named, one name is plenty, no pressure. May travel in the same message as the testimonial ask or separately; recommend which and say why.
5. **Door-open note** (client-facing draft, under 100 words): thank-you, what staying in touch looks like, a real reason and rough date to reconnect. Plus an internal line: date flagged to the Admin & Ops register, roster row updated.

Each client-facing part carries an approval line: "Ready to send? Nothing goes until you say so."

## 11. What good looks like

**Good example (annotated), founder closing a six-month consulting engagement.**

> **Recap excerpt:** "When we started in January you told us the team was spending every Friday rebuilding the same report by hand. [1] As of last month that report runs itself, and you told Priya it saved her 'most of a day, every week'. [2] The one thing we did not get to was the supplier dashboard; that was on the open list from our first call and never in scope, so it is yours to pick up whenever it makes sense. [3]"
>
> **Referral ask excerpt:** "One small ask, with a genuine no-worries if not: is there one other operations lead you know wrestling with the same reporting grind we just fixed? A name and an intro is plenty." [4]

1. The problem in the client's own words from the brief, not the consultant's framing. Peak-end logic: the recap re-lands the peak right before the ending.
2. The result is a documented quote from the client, traceable, not an invented metric.
3. The section 4 open item named honestly as never-agreed scope. Low self-orientation: honesty about the edge builds more trust than a padded win list.
4. Specific ("operations lead", "reporting grind"), one name, real easy out, per the Sales referral honesty rules and Schmitt et al.'s case that this is the highest-value lead there is.

Across the three audiences: the **founder** above; a **professional** closes an internal project with the same recap-handover-ask shape, where the "referral" is an introduction to the next team with the same problem; in **real life**, a departing treasurer hands over the books, a one-page "how this year actually went", and a warm intro to a suggested successor.

**Bad example (named failure mode: the extractive ending).**

> "Hi! As your project wraps up, we'd love a 5-star review, here's the link! We can offer 10% off future work for a glowing testimonial. Also, could you send us 5 contacts who might need our services? P.S. we'll get the final files over at some point."

Failure mode: extraction before service. It asks before it gives (handover an afterthought), incentivises the testimonial's content (fabrication by another name), specifies the sentiment ("glowing", "5-star"), makes a bulk contact grab instead of one specific honest ask, and leaves the ending, the part memory keeps, as a sales pitch. The skill must refuse this shape and run the order that works: give everything back, recap honestly, then ask once, warmly, and leave the door open.
